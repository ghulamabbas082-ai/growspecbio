Planning Framework

Propagation performance is not only about germination. The operational goal is a repeatable handover of uniform young plants into the next production stage.

01

Design around the handover point

Plan propagation backward from the transplant requirement. Define target plant age, root condition, canopy size and batch quantity required by the downstream production area.

02

Keep batches synchronized

Uniform sowing, moisture, temperature, lighting and irrigation help trays reach the transplant window together. Mixed development stages make labour and production scheduling harder to control.

03

Treat the root zone as a production variable

Root access, oxygenation, moisture delivery, nutrient concentration and hygiene influence early-stage consistency. The system should make inspection and routine maintenance practical.

04

Match trays and lighting to crop stage

Seedling, microgreens and young leafy-green stages can require different tray density and canopy spacing. Lighting should support the intended stage rather than simply maximizing intensity.

05

Connect propagation capacity to production capacity

The propagation area should supply the main production farm at the required batch frequency. Too little capacity creates empty production positions; too much can create holding time and uneven transplant age.
