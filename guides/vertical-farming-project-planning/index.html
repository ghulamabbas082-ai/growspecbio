Planning Framework

A commercial indoor farm should be engineered from crop and operating targets backward, not assembled from equipment lists forward.

01

Define crop and market first

Crop, cultivar, harvest stage, packaging format, selling price and customer requirements shape nearly every downstream engineering decision. A farm built before these questions are clear can be difficult to operate efficiently.

02

Translate sales demand into production flow

Work backward from weekly or monthly saleable output. Include propagation, transplanting, crop cycle, harvest, cleaning, losses and planned downtime to estimate planting sites, growing area and batch frequency.

03

Confirm site and utilities early

Electrical service, water quality, drainage, ambient climate, ceiling height, loading access and equipment space can change the system layout. Check them before final equipment selection.

04

Design climate and crop systems together

Lighting, density, irrigation, transpiration, airflow, HVAC and dehumidification affect each other. Treat them as one controlled-environment system rather than separate purchases.

05

Build the operating model before procurement

Staffing, maintenance, nutrient use, cleaning, spare parts, electricity and logistics should be modelled alongside CAPEX. Technical feasibility still needs a realistic operating plan and market route.
