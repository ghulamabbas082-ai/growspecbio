Planning Framework

Commercial microgreens production becomes easier to manage when demand, sowing, germination, grow-out, harvest and sanitation are planned as one operating cycle.

01

Plan demand before choosing capacity

Start with the number of punnets or trays required per week, crop mix and harvest days. Capacity should follow real menu, retail or customer demand rather than the maximum number of shelves available.

02

Separate germination from grow-out

Many microgreens workflows include a dark or germination stage before crops move under lights. Time in each stage affects how many batches can be in process at the same time and therefore affects true capacity.

03

Use staggered batches

Staggered sowing can create a more regular harvest flow and reduce the risk of one large batch maturing at once. The correct interval depends on crop, temperature, light, target harvest stage and actual demand.

04

Sanitation is part of production planning

Tray cleaning, seed handling, water quality, tool hygiene and crop inspection should be built into the routine. High-density short-cycle crops can magnify small operating inconsistencies.

05

Connect growing to point of use

For restaurants, hotels and retail, the system should fit service access, replenishment, cleaning and harvest workflow. A compact cabinet can shorten the distance between production and use, while routine determines consistency.
